Occupational therapy · Sarjapur

Building comfort, coordination and everyday independence.

For children, everyday occupations include playing, learning, dressing, eating and joining family or school routines. Occupational therapy looks at the skills and sensory needs that affect participation in those activities.

Ask about an OT assessment

Parents often contact us about

  • Sensory sensitivities or seeking behaviours
  • Difficulty with fine or gross motor skills
  • Challenges with dressing, feeding or self-care routines
  • Difficulty sitting, attending or joining activities
  • Coordination, play or school-participation concerns

What occupational therapy may work on

Sensory processing

Understanding how sensory input affects comfort, regulation and participation in everyday settings.

Motor skills

Developing relevant fine-motor, gross-motor, coordination and postural skills through meaningful activity.

Self-care

Building practical skills for dressing, eating, toileting and other routines where support is needed.

Play and participation

Supporting attention, engagement, play and participation in home, school and community routines.

What happens before therapy starts?

We discuss your concerns and your child's daily routines, strengths and relevant history. Assessment may include observation of movement, sensory responses, coordination, play and functional skills. Recommendations are explained with practical goals that connect to everyday life.

Questions parents ask about occupational therapy

Does every sensory concern need occupational therapy?

No. Sensory preferences are common. Assessment helps determine whether sensory or motor differences are significantly affecting participation and whether OT support is appropriate.

Will therapy only happen in the OT gym?

No. Equipment can be useful, but goals should connect to real-life participation. Home and school routines may be discussed so skills are meaningful beyond the therapy room.

Can OT support school readiness?

It may help when motor, sensory, attention or self-care skills are affecting classroom participation. The relevant goals depend on the individual child.

How are parents involved?

Parents help identify the routines that matter most and can discuss strategies that are realistic to use at home.
