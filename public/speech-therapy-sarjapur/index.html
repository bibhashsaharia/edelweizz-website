Speech & language therapy · Sarjapur

Helping children communicate in ways that work for them.

Speech and language therapy can support how a child understands language, expresses needs and ideas, develops speech clarity, and connects during play and everyday routines. At Edelweizz, therapy begins with understanding the child—not with a fixed programme.

Ask about a speech assessment

Parents often contact us about

  • Limited spoken words or delayed language
  • Difficulty understanding instructions or questions
  • Speech that is hard for others to understand
  • Difficulty expressing needs, ideas or feelings
  • Concerns about social communication or conversation

What speech therapy may work on

Understanding language

Following everyday directions, understanding questions, concepts and language used at home or school.

Expressing needs and ideas

Developing words, phrases, sentences or other useful communication methods that help the child participate.

Speech clarity

Working on relevant speech sounds and patterns when clarity makes communication difficult.

Social communication

Supporting communication during play, interaction, turn-taking and everyday social situations where appropriate.

What happens before therapy starts?

We first discuss what you are noticing, your child's strengths and relevant history. A speech-language assessment may look at understanding, expression, speech and communication in age-appropriate contexts. Findings are explained in everyday language, and any recommended goals or session frequency are discussed with you.

Questions parents ask about speech therapy

My child understands us but is not speaking much. Should we wait?

There is no single answer based only on age or number of words. If you are concerned, an assessment can help clarify your child's current communication skills and whether support is useful now.

Does a child need a diagnosis before speech therapy?

No. Families can contact us because of a communication concern even without a diagnosis. The professional can discuss what assessment information is relevant.

Will parents know what is being worked on?

Yes. Goals should be understandable and connected to everyday communication. Parents can share observations and ask how to support useful practice at home.

How often are speech therapy sessions needed?

Frequency depends on the assessment, goals, child and family circumstances. We discuss a practical recommendation rather than using one schedule for every child.
