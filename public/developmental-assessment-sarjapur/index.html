Developmental assessment · Sarjapur

Start with understanding, not assumptions.

A developmental assessment can help organise concerns about communication, movement, learning, behaviour, play or everyday skills. The purpose is to understand the child's current strengths and support needs and discuss sensible next steps.

Ask about an assessment

Parents often contact us when

  • Speech or communication seems delayed
  • Play, interaction or learning feels different from peers
  • Sensory or motor concerns affect daily life
  • Behaviour or routines are becoming difficult
  • School or another professional has suggested an assessment

What the assessment process may include

Parent conversation

Your concerns, observations, developmental history and the child's strengths.

Observation and relevant measures

The professional explores skills relevant to the referral area using appropriate observation or assessment methods.

Clear explanation

Findings are discussed in understandable language, including what is going well and where support may help.

Next-step planning

You can discuss whether therapy, monitoring, school input or another professional's opinion is appropriate.

Questions parents ask about assessment

Do we need a diagnosis before booking an assessment?

No. A concern or question is enough to start a conversation. The team can guide you toward the relevant type of assessment.

What should we bring?

Bring relevant previous reports or school observations if you have them. You do not need to collect documents before making the first enquiry.

Does an assessment automatically mean therapy?

No. The purpose is to understand the situation. Recommendations depend on the findings and your child's actual needs.

Can one assessment answer every developmental question?

Not always. Different concerns may require input from different professionals. The team should explain when another assessment or specialist opinion is useful.
